How to use Setu

Setu is a bridge practice table. You bid hands with a computer partner who plays your system, and afterwards it shows you how the hand should have gone.

1. Opening Setu on the MacBook

Setu lives on the internet, like a website. You open it with Safari, the program for looking at websites. You need the internet the first time.

  1. Look at the row of small pictures along the bottom edge of the screen. This row is called the Dock. If you cannot see it, move the pointer to the very bottom of the screen and it will slide up.
  2. Find the picture of a blue compass. That is Safari. Click it once.
  3. A window opens. At the top of the window, in the middle, is a long grey box. Click once inside that box.
  4. Type this address exactly, with no spaces: adityagovilkar-debug.github.io/setu
  5. Press the return key (the wide key on the right of the keyboard).

After a moment you will see a bridge hand and a bidding box. That is Setu.

To click on a MacBook: press down on the flat pad below the keyboard until you feel a small click. To move the page up and down: rest two fingers on the pad and slide them up or down.

2. Keeping Setu where you can find it

Do this once, and you will never need to type the address again.

  1. Open Setu in Safari as in part 1.
  2. Move the pointer to the very top edge of the screen. A row of words appears: Safari, File, Edit, View, and so on.
  3. Click the word File. A list drops down.
  4. Click Add to Dock… in that list.
  5. A small box appears. Click the Add button.

A new picture — a dark square with white dots — is now in the Dock at the bottom of the screen. From now on, click that picture once to open Setu.

If there is no “Add to Dock” in the list: click the word Bookmarks at the top of the screen, then Add Bookmark…, then Add. Next time, open Safari, click Bookmarks, and click Setu.

Once you have opened Setu on a day when the internet was working, it keeps a copy on the MacBook. After that it will open even without the internet.

3. Setu on the Android phone

  1. Open Chrome (the round red, yellow, green and blue picture).
  2. Tap the box at the top and type: adityagovilkar-debug.github.io/setu Then tap the arrow or Go on the keyboard.
  3. Tap the three dots at the top right corner.
  4. Tap Add to Home screen (it may say Install app). Tap Add or Install.

Setu now has its own picture among your other apps.

Important: the phone and the MacBook do not share anything. Changes you make to your system on one will not appear on the other. It is simplest to make your changes on the MacBook and treat the phone as a place to practise. Part 9 shows how to carry your system across if you want to.

4. Bidding a hand

Along the left side (on the phone: along the bottom) are five words: Table, System, Lab, Review, Progress. Table is where you play. Review is not built yet.

At the Table:

  • Your cards are the row of thirteen at the bottom. You are always South.
  • Partner sits opposite and is played by the computer, using your system. West and East are also the computer.
  • The auction is the grid in the middle. A ? means it is your turn.
  • The bidding box is the grid of bids on the right (on the phone: further down). Bids that are not allowed are faded.
  1. When you see the ?, click the bid you want in the bidding box, or click Pass.
  2. The other three players bid straight away, and the ? comes back when it is your turn again.
  3. Carry on until three players pass in a row. The hand is then finished.

Useful things while bidding

  • Click any bid in the auction to read what it means in your system. This appears under “What that bid means”.
  • Shown so far tells you what partner and each opponent have promised up to now.
  • What does my card say? is a button under the bidding box. Click it if you are stuck: it tells you the bid your own system gives for your hand.
  • Deal me, at the top, lets you ask for a kind of hand: one where you open, where partner opens, where you open 1NT, where the opponents open, and so on.
  • New deal throws the hand away and deals another.

5. After the hand

When the bidding ends, Setu turns all four hands face up and works out the result. This takes a second or two.

  • Result tells you the contract, how many tricks it makes if everyone plays perfectly, your score, and the best score your side could have had.
  • Tricks each side can take is a small table showing what every contract would make.
  • Your calls against your card counts how many of your bids matched your own system.

In the auction, some of your bids may now have a coloured line under them:

  • An amber line means your system gives a different bid. Click it to see which, and why.
  • A purple line means your system has no rule for that situation, so you were on your own. These are worth noting: they are gaps in the system.

Then click Next deal, or Bid this deal again to try the same hand another way.

The result assumes everyone can see all four hands and plays perfectly. Real play is less exact. Use it to judge the contract, not to worry about one trick.

6. Changing your system

Click System. This is your convention card. Partner plays exactly what is written here.

Switching a convention on or off

Under Conventions each line has a small switch on the right. Click the line to turn it on or off. Partner changes at once, and a fresh hand is dealt at the Table.

Adding an agreement of your own

  1. Click Add a rule card at the top right.
  2. After: type the bidding so far. For example 1H means partner opened one heart. Put an opponent's bid in brackets: 1S (2H). Leave it empty for an opening bid. Use C, D, H, S for the suits and NT for no-trumps.
  3. Bid: the bid this rule is about, for example 3NT.
  4. Points: the fewest and the most points. Leave “to” empty for “or more”.
  5. Suit lengths: only fill in the suits that matter. “from 5” means five or more. “from 3, to 3” means exactly three.
  6. Meaning: say in your own words what the bid tells partner.
  7. Read the line that begins Reads as. If it says what you intended, click Save rule card.

Your own rule cards always win over the standard system. To change or remove one, use the Change and Remove buttons on the card.

Trying an idea safely

  1. Before you change anything, click Save a version and give it a name, such as “Before trying Bergen”.
  2. Make your change and practise with it at the Table.
  3. If you do not like it, come back to System and click Restore next to the version you saved.

Looking something up

At the bottom of the System page is The whole card. Type a piece of bidding into the box, for example 1NT 2C, and it shows every rule your system has from that point on.

7. Checking your system for gaps

Setu can play through thousands of hands with your system and tell you where it has no answer. These are the places where you and a partner could misunderstand each other at the table.

  1. Click System.
  2. Click Check this system at the top right. Setu deals and bids ten thousand hands. This takes a few seconds.
  3. The Critic's report appears at the top of the page.

Reading the report

  • Bids made on judgment is how often a bid had to be made with no rule to follow. Lower is better.
  • Each gap names a piece of bidding, how often it comes up, and what the computer chose when it had to guess. A red label Hole when they bid means the gap appears after the opponents have bid or doubled.
  • Show me a hand shows a real hand where the gap happened. Bid this deal at the Table takes you there to try it yourself.
  • Write a rule for this opens a new rule card with the bidding already filled in. Add the hand and the bid, and save it. That gap is now covered.
  • If you have your own rule cards, the report says how often each one is used, what partner would have bid without it, and whether two of your cards ask for different bids on the same hand.
  • At the bottom it lists how often each convention was used. A convention marked rare — easy to forget comes up so seldom that a partnership may not remember it when it matters.

The report shows the most frequent gaps first. There are always many very rare ones; you do not need to fill those. Once you change your card, press Check this system again to see the new picture.

8. Testing an idea in the Lab

The Lab answers the question: is my idea better than what I play now? It bids the same hands twice — once with your old card, once with the new one — and compares the results. Because both versions get exactly the same cards, luck cancels out.

  1. Click System, then Save a version. Give it a name, for example “Before semi-forcing”. Click OK.
  2. Now make your change: switch a convention, or add or change a rule card.
  3. Click Lab.
  4. A will be the version you just saved. B will be My card now. Leave them like that.
  5. Under Deals where, pick the kind of hand your idea is about. For an idea about responding to a major, pick We open 1♥ or 1♠. If unsure, leave Any deal.
  6. Leave 2,000 deals and Matchpoints, and click Run the test. It may take up to a minute. Click Stop if you want to end early.

Reading the result

  • The first line gives the verdict: which version scores better, or No clear winner.
  • Bid differently says on how many deals the change made any difference at all. Only those deals count.
  • B's result is B's share of the matchpoints on those deals. 50% means no difference. The number after ± is the margin of error: if the result is closer to 50% than that, the Lab cannot yet tell the two apart.
  • Each dot is one deal: green where B did better, red where A did better, grey where the score was the same.
  • Biggest gains and Biggest losses list the deals that mattered most. Click one to see all four hands and both auctions side by side.

If the Lab says too few deals or no clear winner, run it again with 5,000 deals, or choose a narrower kind of deal. A small change often makes a difference only on a few hands in a hundred.

9. Keeping a safe copy

Your system is stored only on the machine you are using. It is wise to keep a copy.

  1. Click System.
  2. Under Keep a copy, click Save to a file. A file called setu-system is put in the Downloads folder.

To bring it back, or to put it on the phone: click Load from a file and choose that file. To get the file to the phone, email it to yourself and open the email on the phone.

10. If something goes wrong

  • The screen looks stuck or strange. Hold down the command ⌘ key and press R. This loads the page afresh. Nothing you saved is lost.
  • “Solving the deal…” does not go away. Wait ten seconds. If it is still there, press command ⌘ and R together.
  • The writing is too small. Hold command ⌘ and press + to make everything bigger. command ⌘ and − makes it smaller.
  • The screen is too dark or too bright. Click Light or Dark at the top right.
  • Red and black suits are hard to tell apart. Click 2 colours at the top right. It changes to four colours: spades black, hearts red, diamonds blue, clubs green.
  • Partner made a bid you do not understand. Click that bid. If it says “the card is silent here — the bot used its own judgment”, your system has no rule for that position, and the computer guessed.
  • Everything has gone back to the beginning. This happens if Safari's stored website data is cleared. Use Load from a file (part 9) to bring your system back.

11. What Setu cannot do yet

  • It is bidding only. You do not play the cards yet.
  • Review is empty. It will go through your real games from Bridge Base Online.
  • The Lab judges each contract as if everyone could see all four hands and played perfectly. That is fair for comparing two versions, but it is not how one real hand goes.
  • The computer partner is a careful club player, not an expert. It follows the system faithfully, but after the first few bids it tends to settle for a sensible contract, so it will miss some slams.
  • It does not double for penalties except when your side clearly has the points, and it never sacrifices.
